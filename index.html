Our biggest tips

Here's the friendly advice we'd give our own best friends. It all comes from a lot of wedding days, and each little thing helps your day feel calmer and your photos turn out even better.

While you're planning

  • We really love a first look. It gives you a quiet moment together, calmer portraits, and your whole social hour with your guests.
  • Consider asking your officiant to announce an unplugged ceremony. A quick note asking guests to put phones away and stay out of the aisle makes such a difference in your processional photos.
  • It helps to learn your bustle before the wedding. If your bridal shop can show a bridesmaid or your mom at your final fitting, have them take a quick video. (We're really experienced with bustles, even the most complicated ones, and we LOVE helping! Same goes for pinning your veil and doing up those pesky dress buttons while you're getting ready. Just ask!)
  • Pick a day-of point person who isn't you. That way vendors, family, and your wedding party have someone to go to with questions, and you get to just enjoy the day.
  • Try not to plan every single minute. A little breathing room leaves space for unexpected moments without throwing the schedule off.
  • Think about where the sun will be during your ceremony. If you're getting married outdoors, it's worth visiting your ceremony spot around the same time of day to see where the light and shadows fall. (We'd love to give our feedback too, if you'd like!)
  • A few clear umbrellas are a great backup. If it rains, they let the light through and look so pretty in photos. Dark or logo umbrellas tend to block it.

Getting ready

  • Gather your details before we arrive. Rings (yes, even the ones the best man will hold), shoes, an extra invitation suite, jewelry, perfume, heirlooms, and vow books. Having them all in one box or bag means we can jump right in.
  • If possible, we love a room with a big window. Natural light is the most flattering, and we'll make any space work.
  • It's a good idea to have someone ready to pin boutonnières. Florists often drop off and head out, so a mom, aunt, or bridesmaid who's practiced once is a lifesaver. Left lapel, stem pointing down. (We're super experienced with these too, so we're happy to help if needed!)
  • Keep a towel handy for your bouquets. Stems sit in water and love to drip on dresses, so a quick pat-dry before photos helps. A vase of water nearby keeps them fresh in between.
  • A button-up or zip-front top is your friend for hair and makeup, so you don't have to pull anything over your head afterward.
  • Do a quick pocket and wrist check. Phones, keys, and wallets out of suit pockets, and hair ties, smart watches, and fitness trackers off.
  • Please eat! Having someone order lunch for everyone getting ready is a game changer. So many couples forget to eat until dinner.

First look & portraits

  • Other first looks are so fun, if you'd like them. Dad, siblings, or the wedding party in their pajamas make for some of our favorite reactions.
  • If you're writing private vows or letters, paper is the way to go. A vow book or handwritten card photographs so much better than reading from a phone.
  • Try holding your bouquet at your belly button rather than your chest. Everyone holds it a little high when they're nervous, and lowering it shows off your dress and arms.
  • We like to leave a little downtime. We build in 30 minutes tucked away before the ceremony so you can eat, breathe, and touch up.

Family formals

  • Give your family a heads-up ahead of time. Letting everyone on the list know when and where to be, and that we'll need their attention for a few minutes, keeps things moving.
  • It helps to have someone who can wrangle. A friend or relative who knows both families can call out the next group.
  • Thinking about a large extended family photo? We often do those right after the ceremony, while everyone is already together. Let's chat if that's something you're interested in!
  • A focused list goes a long way. When everyone is there and paying attention, each smaller group takes about 1 to 2 minutes.
  • We usually try to photograph grandparents last so it's easier for them, but let us know if you'd prefer to do that differently.

Ceremony

  • You might ask your officiant to step aside for the kiss. That way nobody is standing right behind you in that photo.
  • Hold the kiss for a few seconds. A quick peck can be over before we've had the chance to capture it.
  • If you want, stop for a second kiss at the end of the aisle on your way out. It makes a really fun photo.

Reception

  • If you can, have your caterer serve us when you're served. That way we're done eating and ready to go when speeches start.
  • Sunset photos only take 10 to 15 minutes. We'll sneak you out during the room flip or right after cake.
  • If you're doing a sparkler exit, 36-inch smokeless sparklers work best, with two or three people ready with lighters. The short ones tend to burn out before you reach the end.
  • We're happy to stage a send-off. If your real exit is after we leave, we'll set one up before we go so you still get those photos.
